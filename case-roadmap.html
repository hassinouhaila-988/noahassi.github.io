Problem

A retail e-commerce change portfolio of 11 requests covering Payments, Checkout & Delivery, Product Data, Personalisation & AI, CRM & Marketing Automation, and Promotions / Omnichannel. Many customer-facing features depended on the same systems, data models, UX/UI, development capacity and business approvals, so building them in the wrong order would mean conflicts and rework.

Approach

The main action was making dependencies visible before committing to a plan:

  • Group by domain: turned 11 change requests into 6 epics, each request becoming a feature.
  • Intra-epic dependencies: for every feature, listed the systems (PSP, PIM, CRM, SAP FE/BE, OMS, WMS, AI engine) and teams (Dev, UX/UI, Marketing, AI) needed end-to-end.
  • Inter-epic dependencies: flagged features that need another request's data first, or compete for the same system or team in one iteration.
  • Classify the risk: team conflicts, data readiness, data quality and required business input, each with a mitigation.

Solution

A compact dependency map and an iteration plan that respects it:

  • Sequenced iterations, for example product data enrichment before comparison and scent profile, abandoned-cart emails before gift reminders.
  • Mitigation per conflict, such as early business involvement for promotions and production-data tests for omnichannel order history.
  • Two features specified in depth, Gift Reminders and Pick-up Points, with use cases, 13 open points and 7 user stories with acceptance criteria.

Outcome and results

Created a dependency-led delivery approach for a complex retail e-commerce change portfolio.

Requests were organised into a coherent dependency model, risks and mitigations were made visible, and a proposed iteration order was established, so teams could start delivering without stepping on each other.
