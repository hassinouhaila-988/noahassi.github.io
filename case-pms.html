Problem

The product, PMS, kept growing project by project, and the backlog for each of the projects incremented in both number and complexity (dependencies with other teams or products). Key projects listed out 40+ backlog items and more with the added complexity of having more than 3 concurrent retail projects competing for one small developer team. Without a shared way of working, unclear requirements turned into rework and delivery delays.

Approach

I treated the backlog and the team's workflow as one problem, and introduced Agile practices step by step:

  • Prioritise Projects by business value: ranked the projects first with the involved stakeholders 
  • Prioritise Backlog within Project: defined backlog priorities and dependencies against agree timilines
  • Plan real capacity: sized each sprint on the team's actual availability instead of wish lists. Derived the capacity based on the estimations given on each item 
  • Allocate by skill and dependency: assigned stories taking in consideration that team members skills and knowledge should be interchanged and shared. Avoiding "silos" knowledge bottlenecks
  • Inspect and adapt: led Planning, Review and Retrospective across all sprints, adjusting the process at each cycle.

    Solution

    A delivery toolkit that made "ready" and "done" unambiguous for everyone:

    • 60+ user stories with clear acceptance criteria, written before each sprint.
    • UAT test plans and A/B tests to validate features with business users before release.
    • Iterative releases with agreed scope per market, instead of large, risky drops.
    • Functional specifications, release notes and operating procedures 

    Outcome and results

    Introduced an Agile delivery workflow that let a fast-growing Promotion Management System scale across projects and markets.

    Development rework dropped by about 15%, team velocity rose by 10%, and delivery delays fell by about 10%, with consistent on-time / before deadline releases .
